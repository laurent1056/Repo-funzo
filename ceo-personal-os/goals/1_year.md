# One-Year Goals

**Set:** February 2026
**Review by:** February 2027

These are your goals for the next twelve months. They should connect directly to your stated priorities and your North Star. Good goals are specific enough to measure, meaningful enough to motivate, and honest enough to be achievable.

---

## Your Priorities (for reference)

1. Getting a new job
2. Being a better husband
3. Being a better dad
4. Being healthy

---

## Goals

### Goal 1: Secure a role that aligns with my experience, values, and lifestyle needs

**Priority:** Getting a new job
**Why it matters:** Financial stability, sense of purpose, professional identity, modeling resilience for my kids.

**What success looks like:**
- I am in a role I chose deliberately — not one I took out of panic
- The compensation covers my Ideal Life number (see `frameworks/ideal_life_costing.md`)
- The work uses my CEO/founder experience in a meaningful way
- The role allows me to be present for my family, not consumed by work

**Key actions:**
- [ ] Complete Ideal Lifestyle Costing to know my floor number
- [ ] Define my non-negotiables for a role (remote/hybrid, travel limits, comp floor)
- [ ] Reach out to 5 people per week in my network — real conversations, not mass emails
- [ ] Update positioning materials (resume, LinkedIn, narrative) to reflect what I want next, not just what I've done
- [ ] Have at least 3 exploratory conversations per week
- [ ] Track job search progress in weekly reviews

**Quarterly milestones:**
- Q1: Active pipeline of 10+ genuine opportunities. Clear narrative about what I'm looking for.
- Q2: Deep conversations with 3-5 serious prospects. At least one offer or advanced process.
- Q3: In a role, or have a clear path to one with a defined timeline.
- Q4: Settled into the new role. Initial 90-day plan completed.

### Goal 2: Measurably strengthen my marriage

**Priority:** Being a better husband
**Why it matters:** This is the most important relationship in your life. Everything else is downstream of this.

**What success looks like:**
- My wife would say things have genuinely improved — not just that I'm trying
- We have regular, protected time together that isn't about logistics
- I listen more than I talk. I ask more than I assume.
- We've had the hard conversations I've been avoiding

**Key actions:**
- [ ] Establish a weekly date — even if it's just a walk or a conversation at home after kids are in bed
- [ ] Ask my wife once a month: "What do you need from me that you're not getting?"
- [ ] Put my phone away during family dinners and evening time — actually away, not face-down
- [ ] Initiate at least one meaningful (non-logistical) conversation per week
- [ ] Write an honest assessment in my weekly review about how I showed up as a husband

**How I'll know it's working:**
- My wife tells me, without prompting, that she notices a change
- We're fighting less about the same recurring issues
- I can point to specific moments each week where I was genuinely present

---

### Goal 3: Be a more present, engaged father

**Priority:** Being a better dad
**Why it matters:** My kids are growing up now, not later. The window for influence and connection is finite.

**What success looks like:**
- I attend their important events — not as an obligation, but because I want to be there
- They come to me with things — problems, stories, questions — because they know I'll listen
- I model healthy behavior: how to handle setbacks, how to take care of yourself, how to treat a partner

**Key actions:**
- [ ] Be physically present for at least one activity or event per child per week
- [ ] Have one real conversation with each kid per week — not about logistics or homework, about their lives
- [ ] Share appropriate amounts of what I'm going through (job search, challenges) so they see that struggle is normal
- [ ] Put away work/phone during family time — no exceptions
- [ ] Track in daily check-ins: "How did I show up for my family today?"

**How I'll know it's working:**
- My kids seek me out for conversation or company
- My spouse notices and comments on my presence
- I can recall specific moments with my kids each week, not just a blur

---

### Goal 4: Build a sustainable health foundation

**Priority:** Being healthy
**Why it matters:** At 51, health is no longer optional or deferrable. It is the infrastructure that everything else depends on.

**What success looks like:**
- I exercise 4+ times per week — consistently, not in bursts
- I sleep 7+ hours most nights
- I have energy throughout the day, not just in the morning
- I've addressed any health issues I've been avoiding (checkups, screenings, nagging problems)

**Key actions:**
- [ ] Schedule and complete a full physical with bloodwork
- [ ] Establish a simple, repeatable exercise routine (not an ambitious program — something I'll actually do)
- [ ] Set a hard shutdown time for screens (9 PM target)
- [ ] Track energy level daily in check-ins
- [ ] Reduce or eliminate habits that drain energy (alcohol, late nights, doom scrolling)
- [ ] Drink more water. (Simple, but real.)

**Quarterly milestones:**
- Q1: Physical completed. Exercise routine established (3x/week minimum). Sleep improving.
- Q2: Exercise at 4x/week. Consistent sleep schedule. Noticeable energy improvement.
- Q3: Health is on autopilot — it's a habit, not a goal. Measurable improvements in energy and fitness.
- Q4: Maintain. This is the new baseline.

## Meta-Goal: Use This System Consistently

**What success looks like:**
- Daily check-ins at least 5 days per week
- Weekly reviews every week, no exceptions
- Quarterly reviews completed within the first week of each quarter
- Memory.md updated regularly with patterns

**This isn't a goal — it's the vehicle for all the other goals.**

---

## Annual Check-In Prompts

At the end of the year, ask yourself:
- Did I achieve these goals, or just talk about them?
- Which goal surprised me — was easier or harder than expected?
- What goal should I have set but didn't?
- Did my priorities shift? If so, was that growth or avoidance?

---

*Review these goals during every quarterly review. Adjust if needed — goals should evolve, not calcify. But be honest about whether changes are strategic adjustments or convenient retreats.*
