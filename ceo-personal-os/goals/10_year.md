# Ten-Year Vision

**Set:** February 2026
**Time Horizon:** 2036 — You'll be 61.
**Review:** Annually, during your annual review. Don't overthink updates — this is a compass, not a plan.

---

## What This Document Is

You can't plan ten years. You know that. You've seen five-year business plans evaporate in six months. So this isn't a plan. It's an answer to one question:

**If I could design the next decade of my life, what would I be building toward?**

You're not committing to specifics. You're choosing a direction. The direction matters. The details will change.

---

## The Long View

### At 61, I want to be someone who...

**...chose well.** The career transition of my early 50s was a turning point, not a setback. I used it to recalibrate. I didn't just find another job — I found work that fit the life I wanted. And when that work evolved, I evolved with it, because I'd learned how to make deliberate choices instead of reactive ones.

**...invested in the right things.** My marriage is deep. My kids are launched — or launching — and they call me because they want to, not because they have to. The relationships I built in my 50s are the ones that sustained me. I didn't sacrifice the people for the work.

**...took care of the machine.** I'm 61 and I'm healthy. Not because I'm lucky — because I made a decision at 51 to stop treating my body like it was optional. I can travel, play with grandkids (if they come), hike, move. My health is a source of freedom, not limitation.

**...knows who he is.** I'm not performing anymore. Not for employers, not for social media, not for old friends who remember the CEO version of me. I know what I value. I know what I'm good at. I know what I'm not. I'm settled — not complacent, but settled.

**...has enough.** Not rich, necessarily — but enough. Enough money that financial decisions aren't driven by fear. Enough time that I'm not always rushing. Enough security that my family is protected. Enough meaning that I don't feel empty.

**...is still curious.** I haven't stopped learning. I haven't stopped being interested in the world. The worst version of 61 is the one who gave up and got comfortable. The best version is still asking questions, still trying things, still growing — just at a pace that's sustainable.

## Domains at 61

### Career / Work
- I'm either in a senior role I find meaningful, running something small of my own, or in a portfolio career (advising, board seats, mentoring)
- I'm not retired unless I chose to be — and if I'm retired, I'm busy with purpose
- My professional reputation is solid, based on decades of real work
- I'm mentoring people younger than me — passing on what I learned

### Marriage
- My wife and I have been through hard things and came out bonded, not broken
- We travel together. We laugh together. We're not just coexisting.
- We've built a life we both chose, not one that just happened to us

### Family
- My kids are independent, functional adults who respect me
- They come home because they want to, not out of obligation
- They saw a father who struggled honestly and handled it with dignity

### Health
- I'm active and capable at 61
- No major preventable health problems
- I prioritized this for a full decade and it shows

### Finances
- Retirement is funded and on track
- No burdensome debt
- Financial decisions are made from abundance, not scarcity
- I've been generous where it mattered

### Inner Life
- I have a sense of peace that I didn't have at 51
- I've done the work — the hard conversations, the reflection, the growth
- I'm not chasing. I'm present.

## One Sentence

If you had to compress your ten-year vision into one sentence:

**In ten years, I want to be ___.**

*Example: "In ten years, I want to be a healthy, present, financially secure man who chose his path and has the relationships to prove it."*

---

## How to Use This

- Read this once a year during your annual review.
- Don't stress about updating it. If it still resonates, leave it. If it doesn't, rewrite the parts that feel off.
- Use it as a decision filter for big choices: "Does this move me toward my ten-year picture or away from it?"
- Share it with your spouse if you're comfortable. Alignment on the long view matters.

---

*The purpose of a ten-year vision isn't to predict the future. It's to make sure you're building something — not just drifting.*
