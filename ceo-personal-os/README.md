# CEO Personal Operating System

A private, single-user personal productivity system for reflection, clarity, and intentional living.

This is not software. It is a system — a set of plain-text files, structured prompts, and thinking frameworks designed to help you run your life with the same rigor you bring to running a company, but without the bureaucracy.

No dashboards. No apps. No productivity theater. Just honest reflection, clear priorities, and calm accountability.

## How It Works

This system lives in a folder on your computer. Every file is plain text (Markdown). You can open and edit them with any text editor, or use Claude Code to walk you through the prompts conversationally.

There are four rhythms:

| Rhythm | Time Required | When |
|-----------|---------------|------|
| Daily | 5 minutes | Every morning or evening |
| Weekly | 20-30 minutes | Sunday evening or Monday morning |
| Quarterly | 60-90 minutes | First week of each quarter |
| Annual | Half day | Last week of December or first week of January |

## Quick Start (Under 15 Minutes)

### Step 1: Read Your North Star (2 minutes)
Open `north_star.md`. Read the placeholder version. Replace it with your own words — even a single sentence about what matters most to you right now.

### Step 2: Skim Your Principles (2 minutes)
Open `principles.md`. These are starter principles. Cross out what doesn't fit. Add what does. You can always come back to this.

### Step 3: Do Your First Daily Check-In (5 minutes)
Open `reviews/daily/`. Copy the template at the top. Fill it in. Save with today's date as the filename (e.g., `2026-02-08.md`). Done.

### Step 4: Glance at the Frameworks (5 minutes)
Browse the `frameworks/` folder. You don't need to complete anything now. Just know they exist. They'll be used during quarterly and annual reviews.

### Step 5: You're Running
Come back tomorrow for another daily check-in. On Sunday, do your first weekly review. That's it.

## How to Use Each Section

### Daily Check-In (`reviews/daily/`)
Create a new file each day (e.g., `2026-02-08.md`). Copy the template from the top of any existing daily file. Fill it in. Five minutes, max. The goal is awareness, not performance.

### Weekly Review (`reviews/weekly/`)
Once a week, create a new file (e.g., `week-2026-02-09.md`). This takes 20-30 minutes. You're looking for patterns: what moved the needle, what was noise, where time leaked.

### Quarterly Review (`reviews/quarterly/`)
Every three months, create a new file (e.g., `Q1-2026.md`). This is where you check alignment between your goals and your actual behavior. Takes about an hour. Be honest.

### Annual Review (`reviews/annual/`)
Once a year, dedicate a half day. Use the frameworks in `frameworks/` and the interview scripts in `interviews/`. Produce a narrative of the past year and set clear intent for the next one.

### Interviews (`interviews/`)
These are guided self-conversations. Read a question, write your answer. No rush. You can do one question per sitting or power through the whole script. They're designed to surface what you already know but haven't articulated.

### Frameworks (`frameworks/`)
Each framework file explains a thinking tool in plain language, then gives you a template to apply it. Use them during quarterly and annual reviews, or whenever you need to think clearly about a specific dimension of your life.

### Goals (`goals/`)
Three time horizons. Start with the 1-year goals — they should connect directly to your four priorities. The 3-year and 10-year files are for vision, not precision.

### Uploads (`uploads/`)
Drop old annual reviews, performance reviews, journal entries, or personal notes into the appropriate subfolder. When you're ready, use Claude Code to summarize them, extract patterns, and update `memory.md`.

### Memory (`memory.md`)
This is the system's long-term memory. It stores patterns detected across your reviews, interviews, and uploaded documents. Over time, it becomes a mirror — showing you your recurring themes, blind spots, and strengths.
