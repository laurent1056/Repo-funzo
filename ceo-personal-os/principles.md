# Operating Principles

These are the rules you run your life by. Not aspirations — principles. The difference is that principles hold even when they're inconvenient.

Review these quarterly. Edit them when they no longer fit. A principle you don't follow isn't a principle — it's a wish.

---

## Current Principles

### 1. Family is the job that doesn't send rejection letters
Career transitions end. Your kids grow up exactly once. When in doubt about where to spend the next hour, choose the people who live in your house.

### 2. Health is infrastructure, not a goal
You don't "get around to" maintaining the foundation of a building. Exercise, sleep, and nutrition are not rewards for productivity — they are the base layer that makes everything else possible.

### 3. Clarity over speed
The worst decisions come from rushing. Before you act, ask: "Do I actually understand what I'm trying to do here?" If the answer is no, stop and think before you move.

### 4. Say the hard thing today
Whether it's a conversation with your spouse, a negotiation with a potential employer, or an honest look in the mirror — the cost of avoidance compounds daily.

### 5. Do fewer things, better
You've run companies. You know that focus beats volume. Apply that same discipline to your personal life. Three real priorities, fully pursued, will outperform twelve half-hearted ones.

### 6. Your reputation is a lagging indicator of your character
Don't manage perceptions. Manage behavior. The rest follows.

### 7. Rest is not laziness
At 51, you've earned the right to stop performing busyness. Rest deliberately. Recover fully. The people who burn out aren't weak — they just forgot that recovery is part of the work.

### 8. The answer is usually in the room
Before hiring a coach, reading another book, or starting another framework — sit quietly and ask yourself what you already know. You usually know more than you think.

---

## How to Use These

- Read them on Monday morning before the week starts.
- When you face a hard decision, scan the list. Does one of these principles point you in a direction?
- During your quarterly review, ask: "Which principle did I live well? Which one did I violate?"
- Add new principles only when you've truly learned something. Remove ones that no longer serve you.

---

*These are starter principles based on your stated priorities. Replace, edit, or rewrite any of them. The best principles are the ones you wrote yourself.*
